How I Work 3+1 cases that show how we can work together

Three completed engagements, one in progress. Different industries, different scales, but the same structural footprint of any organisation trying to do meaningful work in complexity, packed with diversity, and demanding KPIs.

Here you can see how I lead strategic projects across departments, how I surface the real challenges and the incentive systems, and how I partner with different departments and functions to fill the gaps and make sure great ideas survive contact with reality in a way traditional consultants cannot.

Change Without a Blueprint

Dentsu iProspect APAC · Singapore · 2018–2019 The Challenge

400-person APAC agency in Singapore. Nine function heads each owning their own strategy. High churn, margins under pressure, scope creep, multiple failed change initiatives. KPIs spanning commercial, delivery, talent, and culture.

The Diagnosis

The voiced problem was process and consistency. The behavioural read revealed the real one: the organisation was only rewarding revenue gain, and success measurement pushed every function toward conflicting KPIs while the organisation didn't clear the conflict of priorities, leaving it to cascade down across teams. This led to situations in which profitability wasn't even accounted for in some client engagements as long as revenue goals were hit.

The Approach

I like a dual workstream approach when starting new. While I run diagnosis I volunteer to work on the hardest problem the teams have (in this case a large demanding client) to earn trust fast, share accountability, and map the real organisation. Both workstreams feed each other and allow me to contribute better and understand the organisation. Within months, the work was cross-functional by necessity. Partnered across functions on the cross-cuts each of them owned a piece of but none could close alone.

At the same time, I was coordinating strategic cross-function projects like implementation of new technologies, staff learning and development, company-wide culture improvement, and building new products and services. I mapped how each team worked, their playbooks, workflows, pain points, handovers with other teams, and the opportunities for automation, new technology, outsourcing, and more strategic and valuable work. After surfacing the real organisation, I co-designed a new operating system, DDO, with inputs from all teams, anchoring it in helping clients by helping those creating the experience for our clients. Tested with one team, scaled across nine. Built feedback loops and empowered champions so it could run without me. The APAC CEO eventually asked me to become interim Chief of Staff to formalise a role I was already doing.

Lasting Outcomes

These outcomes cannot be uniquely attributed to my work but to a combination of efforts across departments. Reduced employee churn by 50% in 6 months · Increased pitch success by 33% while reducing number of pitches · From 28% unprofitable clients to 0 · 1 day/week saved per employee in removed inefficiencies.

DDO still in use after I left. Several function heads took it to their next organisations. Including the most contrarian stakeholders.

How I Would Do It Today With AI +

AI would let me assess and process data and feedback in a fraction of the time. Creating custom platforms for the purpose and even maintaining a live feed. Reducing time for diagnosis and mapping by 2/3. DDO then could have been a platform every team could use instead of useful documentation, and every artifact could live online, connected, instead of in documents.

I would also use AI to quickly build benchmark workflows and analyse current workflows or client engagements against them, to see where they differ and improve them. It would also allow us to create new services and products, taking them to market at speed, while maintaining consistency in our strategy and design approach.

Innovation from First Principles

Rainmaking / Engie Factory · Singapore · 2019–2020 The Challenge

Build an open innovation lab and program for Engie Factory in APAC (global energy multinational). New sector, no pre-existing methodology, unprecedented project, six months, team of three. The VPs we needed to collaborate with to identify new opportunities and build pilots had no P&L incentive to participate. I had no hierarchy to ask them, I had to lead by influence — good ideas or methodology alone wouldn't move them.

The Diagnosis

Incentive misalignment at VP level. The strategic logic of the lab and the strategic logic of each business unit weren't connected. No pilot would survive until that changed. They were asked to meet KPIs that didn't include any innovation, only commercial and budget-driven goals. But since the CEO wanted them to also run innovation without adapting the incentive systems or KPIs, some were genuinely interested, some were sceptical, and some openly tried to avoid it as a distraction from their actual KPIs — hoping the initiative would go away if proved unsuccessful fast.

The Approach

Designed and ran the program with the Rainmaking team, partnering with Engie Factory's leadership as primary client. Making an example with the VPs more inclined to collaborate. When overall VP engagement stalled, I took the elevator to the top floor, ambushed Engie's CEO to first convince him I actually worked there, then to propose each business unit tie a percentage of next year's revenue to a product or service line that didn't exist the year before as a clear, unmistakable KPI. Our lab then became a strategic partner, no longer a nice to have. VP engagement followed.

Productised the methodology as Pilot ID and Co-Pilot. To identify and test five concurrent pilots working with technology from startups from all over the world for new use cases aligned with Engie's 2030 SDG agenda. Building business models and agreements between startups and corporate. Adapted to remote delivery mid-COVID.

Lasting Outcomes

Built on a commercial model with skin in the game — profit only if we succeeded.

1,000+ startups screened · 5 concurrent pilots · SGD 4M revenue approved · 33% pilot success rate

Our custom Pilot ID and Co-Pilot approaches were adopted by Rainmaking for other clients and geographies.

How I Would Do It Today With AI +

Reducing overall time for Pilot ID by 50% on account of screening startups faster. Increasing the number of modelled opportunities using AI models with known database APIs instead of human calibration and research. Incrementing the pilot identification and accuracy, as well as the tested solutions, resulting in upstream increase of success from 33% to potentially over 50%. And from SGD 4 million in future revenue or saved costs to 6. We would also have been able to productise it as a platform for subsequent cycles, with the same or different clients, building human and AI in the loop workflows and efficiencies.

Building in Complexity

Apple · Singapore · 2020–2023 The Challenge

Build a global program from scratch during the pandemic as Head of Operations and Organisational Design — from 20 people in 10 countries to 350 across 40, serving 12 Apple products and 4 business lines, in under three years, while budgets grew 10× (4M → 40M per quarter). No operating model. No measurement system. No internal tech. 14 regulated markets, with compliance and local institutional requirements as a constant, and with the high expectations Apple has for design of solutions and ways of working.

The Diagnosis

Building from zero in such complexity and ambiguity in the middle of the pandemic, and at that scale and pace, didn't allow for perfection. It was like building a scale-up backed by Apple instead of by a VC. Multiple workstreams, sometimes conflicting, so I realised a sequential operating model would always be lagging behind reality and would have us reacting constantly. We needed operational coherence over perfection.

The Approach

I wanted to maximise shared context and facilitate access to information, prioritising coherence over perfection.

I was one of the core members of the leadership team, but my role was building the infrastructure and designing the operating conditions that made it scalable as complexity grew with Apple standards: governance, incentive structures, automation, workflow, playbooks, tech, training architecture, the full employee lifecycle, service levels, supporting strategy and delivery teams.

This included partnering with the heads of all 9 departments on operating model design, adoption, and continuous improvement to meet our evolving goals and KPIs each quarter.

Onboarded 400+ people. Offboarded 50+. Ran 100+ training sessions. Introduced AI automation through custom-built workflow systems, building tech bespoke to our workflows and needs.

Built a dual project portfolio seed. We had so many initiatives it was impossible to cater to all of them at the same time, so we prioritised our portfolio the same as a product backlog — thinking of our organisation as a product meant to operate in a certain way, provide certain experience and services to different stakeholders, including the very people inside the organisation. Then tackled the top priorities a few at a time, reassessing the backlog often to keep us nimble, but also acted behaviourally, looking at the overall pattern and the single simplest behavioural intervention that could produce the most positive snowball effect towards our KPIs. We called it Cornerstone. This was optimised for asynchronous work and coherence over perfection.

I managed day-to-day operations, the Cornerstone program, and the portfolio of strategic initiatives across 12 product lines, 4 business lines, 9 departments, and dozens of platform vendors — which contributed to a sustained 85–110% KPI quarterly as we 10×'d in 3 years.

Lasting Outcomes

20→350 people in 3 years · 40 countries · 10× budget expansion · 85–110% KPI sustained results

A New School for Unconventional Learners

Singular Solving · Spain · 2024–present · In progress The Challenge

Design and launch a new Masters outside conventional university logic, built for neurodivergent, gifted, and unconventional learners. For the students, parents and teachers that values-driven institutions most want to serve but find hardest to fit into existing models. Optimising for critical thinking, integrity, and the capacity to act under accelerating complexity rather than credential accumulation. They asked me to contribute to the vision and execution, giving me a blank canvas, a deadline, and the freedom to choose the team to work with.

The Diagnosis

Most educational design starts from the curriculum and works outward to the student. For the population this Masters serves, that order produces predictable failure — the student is either flattened to fit the model, or judged unfit for it. We also didn't want to come up with alternative ways of learning that we would put inside 20th-century education models, which is what most innovation in education ends up doing — creating the perfect scenario for the container to eat the content. We wanted to start by committing to the challenge: how do we build systems navigators in complexity that lead with integrity and courage.

The Approach

We asked students, parents, and teachers. And we researched how others are solving this challenge elsewhere. We also deeply investigated what neurodivergent and gifted profiles need — broadly 2 to 10% of the population. Building a bridge between the vision and execution alongside Singular Solving's leadership.

The work spans curriculum architecture, learning environment design, faculty selection criteria, assessment methodology, and the institutional conditions that have to hold for any of it to survive contact with reality.

I also started teaching Systems Transformation beyond default optimisation logics and current IB standards to test it as part of the Masters' core content.

Where It Stands Now

Pivoting to full school build instead of just an alternative Masters program. In active design and in the process of buying a school in Spain to teach from Early Years to Year 12, plus the Masters program.

This is the most important work I've been trusted with — and the closest in shape to what I imagine contributing to UWC with.

Chapter 01

Finding a call

Curiosity beyond curriculum.

A small medieval city

Spain · Late 90s

As a teenager I grew up in a small medieval city in the north of Spain called León. Other than playing basketball at national level, reading and writing a lot, I also used to skip English or Maths support lessons to go to the public library to investigate and write for hours about the age of explorers, antimatter, or how the brain works. I believe kids can only dream of becoming what they know exists, and that is why I chose Investigative Journalism from the narrow window I had to the world.

University and early success

Spain · 2002–2007

I worked in a newspaper during university while studying Journalism and Law in two different universities, one in the morning, the other in the afternoon. Passing everything proved too much, so I dropped out of Law after 2 years.

I thought I would be a journalist for life, because early on I authored award-winning investigations, including nation-wide work collaborating with the Attorney General on education that led to new laws and an improved system. This is the beginning of a 15-year pursuit of better education as a side quest that I am now gradually trying to make my main story.

I would spend half my small salary buying second-hand books, and the second half handing over at home. I also used all my leave to do the most UWC thing I could do at the time — getting grants to participate in multicultural ethnographic expeditions with students from different countries, or getting a grant to film a 3-month documentary in Cuba. I didn't know it then, but those instincts became the foundation for everything I do.

Chapter 02

Chapter 05

Elevated work and high stakes

Designing the operating system, not just the project.

Parachuted into a new system

Singapore · 2018–2019

After 2 years in London we wanted to move back to Asia. And I wanted to elevate the type of innovation and transformation work I was doing, working at the level where I could influence the system incentives. I learned our global innovation lab was in Singapore, so I sold my comic collection and borrowed money to fly to Singapore and pitch for a role that didn't exist.

Once in Singapore, our APAC CEO asked me to design and build a new distributed operating system, introduce strategic project management best practices, increase innovation, implement new technology, and bring together a fragmented leadership team — filling the gaps to build new products, improve the culture, and develop cross-agency services. The agency was 2,000 people across APAC, 400 in Singapore. High churn, siloed teams, weak margins, no consistent commercial, delivery, or operations method, and high scepticism after failed change attempts.

My approach, different to traditional consulting teams, is a dual work-stream: volunteering time to work on the team's hardest challenge, sharing the pain and accountability, while conducting organisational diagnostic. They both feed each other and eliminate the most common biases Big 4 have: overuse of off-the-shelf solutions, and drawing conclusions based on opinions instead of behaviours.

That gap between what a system claims to be and need, and what the behaviours actually reward and explain, is what makes the difference between strategy and interventions that work or don't.

I redesigned our workflows and increased pitch success: 18% → 32%. Profit margins: 8% → 18%, and culture approval. While reducing annual churn: 48% → 26% and shipping new products and services to market 1.8× faster. I also led the build of a cross-agency product and service foundry no other agency in the region had, and was promoted to APAC Business Transformation Lead and interim Chief of Staff before global layoffs sent me home.
